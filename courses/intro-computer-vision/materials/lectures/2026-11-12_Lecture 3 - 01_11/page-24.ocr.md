# Lines and points in $\mathbb{P}^2$

* The set of equivalence classes of vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3 - (0,0,0)^T$ forms the projective space $\mathbb{P}^2$, where $\underline{\text{lines and points}}$ are represented by homogeneous coordinates

* Points are dual to lines in $\mathbb{P}^2$

* $\underline{\text{Duality principle.}}$ To any theorem of 2 dimensional projective geometry there corresponds a dual theorem, which may be derived by interchanging the roles of points and lines in the original theorem.

* $\underline{\text{Example.}}$ The statement "Two lines intersect each other in one point" is dual to "For any two points there is one line going through them both"

# Lines and points in $\mathbb{P}^2$

## Intersection and incidence

* The intersection of two lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ is the point 
$$p = l_1 \times l_2$$

* The line through two points $p_1$ and $p_2$ is
$$l = p_1 \times p_2$$

[diagram: A 3D-like parallelogram showing vectors $\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$, the angle $\theta$ between them, their cross product $\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}$, and its magnitude $|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}|$]

## Proof (of the first claim)

$$(l_1 \times l_2) \perp l_1 \Rightarrow (l_1 \times l_2)^T l_1 = 0 \Rightarrow p \in l_1$$
$$(l_1 \times l_2) \perp l_2 \Rightarrow (l_1 \times l_2)^T l_2 = 0 \Rightarrow p \in l_2$$

$\Rightarrow p$ is the intersection point
