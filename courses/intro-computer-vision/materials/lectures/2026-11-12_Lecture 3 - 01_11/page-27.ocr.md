Line at infinity = vanishing line

- Parallel lines intersect at an ideal point:
  $l_1 = (a, b, c)^T$ and $l_2 = (a, b, c')^T$ intersect at
  $p = l_1 \times l_2 \propto (b, -a, 0)$

- Every set of parallel lines of different orientation
  intersects at a different point at infinity
- The complete set of ideal points
  is called the line at infinity
- The line at infinity is indeed
  a line = a plane of rays through
  the origin (the $Z = 0$ plane)
- A line at infinity can be thought of the set of
  "directions" of lines in the plane
- Algebraically, $l_\infty = (0, 0, 1)^T$
